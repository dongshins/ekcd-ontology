---

## 1. Purpose

EKCD v1.2.0 is a non-breaking vocabulary-expansion and semantic-model refinement release focused on the representation of interpretive assertions in historical and digital-humanities research.

The principal change is the separation of two dimensions that were previously combined in the controlled values `ekcd:Direct`, `ekcd:Probable`, and `ekcd:Contextual`:

1. the **basis on which an interpretation is made**, and
2. the **epistemic confidence assigned to that interpretation**.

Version 1.2.0 introduces an explicit interpretive-basis axis while restricting the confidence axis to confidence alone. This allows, for example, an inferential interpretation to be assigned high confidence, or an interpretation based on direct evidence to be assigned medium confidence when identification, reading, or contextual circumstances remain uncertain.

The release also clarifies the methodological relationship between EKCD's lightweight interpretive-assertion model and **CRMinf**. EKCD acknowledges conceptual overlap with CRMinf in the modeling of argumentation, inference, belief, and evidential provenance, but does not import CRMinf or assert formal ontology alignment with CRMinf classes.

The existing EKCD namespace and previously published vocabulary IRIs are preserved.

## 4. Two-axis interpretive model

### 4.1 Interpretive basis

Version 1.2.0 introduces:

* `ekcd:InterpretiveBasisType`
* `ekcd:hasInterpretiveBasis`

`ekcd:hasInterpretiveBasis` associates an `ekcd:InterpretiveAssertion` with the type of basis on which the interpretation is made.

Three controlled values are introduced:

* `ekcd:DirectEvidenceBasis`
* `ekcd:InferentialBasis`
* `ekcd:ContextualBasis`

These values describe the **epistemic or evidential basis of an interpretation**, not the researcher's degree of confidence in the resulting assertion.

#### `ekcd:DirectEvidenceBasis`

This value is intended for interpretations grounded primarily in directly attested or explicitly observable evidence, such as an explicit statement in a source, an inscription, a heading, a directly identifiable visual feature, or another form of evidence that bears directly on the asserted relation.

The presence of direct evidence does not by itself imply high epistemic confidence. Reading, identification, dating, attribution, or other interpretive uncertainties may remain.

#### `ekcd:InferentialBasis`

This value is intended for interpretations established primarily through inference from one or more items of evidence rather than through a single directly attested relation.

An inferential basis may nevertheless support high confidence when independent evidence converges strongly on the same interpretation.

#### `ekcd:ContextualBasis`

This value is intended for interpretations established primarily through historical, textual, visual, institutional, social, political, spatial, ritual, or other contextual relationships.

A contextual basis does not imply low confidence. Contextual reasoning may be strongly supported when the relevant historical context is well established.

---

### 4.2 Epistemic confidence

`ekcd:hasInterpretiveConfidence` is retained but is clarified in v1.2.0 as representing **epistemic confidence only**.

Three new controlled values are introduced:

* `ekcd:HighConfidence`
* `ekcd:MediumConfidence`
* `ekcd:LowConfidence`

These values represent the researcher's assessed degree of confidence in an `ekcd:InterpretiveAssertion`.

They do not indicate whether the interpretation is based on direct, inferential, or contextual evidence.

---

### 4.3 Independence of the two dimensions

Interpretive basis and epistemic confidence are modeled as independent dimensions.

For example, the following combinations are all valid:

| Interpretive basis | Epistemic confidence | Possible interpretation                                                                |
| ------------------ | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Direct evidence    | High                 | The evidence directly supports the assertion and identification is secure.             |
| Direct evidence    | Medium               | The evidence is direct, but reading, attribution, or identification remains uncertain. |
| Inferential        | High                 | Multiple independent pieces of evidence converge strongly on the inferred relation.    |
| Inferential        | Medium               | The inference is plausible, but alternative explanations remain possible.              |
| Contextual         | High                 | The interpretation depends on context, but that context is strongly documented.        |
| Contextual         | Low                  | The interpretation is contextually plausible but weakly supported.                     |

A typical v1.2.0 assertion may therefore be represented as:

```turtle
sok:Assertion001
    a ekcd:InterpretiveAssertion ;
    ekcd:assertsSubject sok:ResourceA ;
    ekcd:assertsPredicate ekcd:correspondsTo ;
    ekcd:assertsObject sok:ResourceB ;
    prov:wasDerivedFrom sok:Source001 ;
    ekcd:hasInterpretiveBasis ekcd:InferentialBasis ;
    ekcd:hasInterpretiveConfidence ekcd:HighConfidence .
```

This separation is intended to support historical and digital-humanities research in which researchers need to distinguish **how an interpretation is supported** from **how strongly they accept that interpretation**.

## 5. Methodological relationship to CRMinf

### 5.1 Conceptual relationship

EKCD's interpretive-assertion model addresses concerns that overlap conceptually with CRMinf, particularly the explicit representation of scholarly interpretation, evidence, inference, belief, and provenance.

CRMinf provides a substantially more detailed model for representing argumentation and inference processes, including reasoning activities, premises, conclusions, belief adoption, and inference logic.

EKCD v1.2.0 instead retains a lightweight project-level pattern intended primarily for historical and digital-humanities datasets in which the immediate modeling requirement is to:

* distinguish source evidence from researcher interpretation;
* identify the subject, predicate, and object of an interpretive assertion;
* trace an assertion to supporting evidence;
* record the basis on which an interpretation is made; and
* record epistemic confidence independently from that basis.

---

### 5.2 Deliberate absence of formal alignment

EKCD v1.2.0 does **not**:

* import CRMinf with `owl:imports`;
* declare `ekcd:InterpretiveAssertion` equivalent to a CRMinf class;
* declare `ekcd:InterpretiveAssertion` as a subclass of a CRMinf class;
* assert `skos:exactMatch` between EKCD and CRMinf terms;
* assert `skos:closeMatch` between EKCD and CRMinf terms.

This absence of formal alignment is deliberate.

It should not be interpreted as a claim that EKCD and CRMinf are conceptually unrelated. Rather, EKCD adopts a lighter modeling commitment appropriate to its intended use in Korean cultural-heritage, historical, and digital-humanities knowledge modeling.

Ontology-level and term-level annotations in v1.2.0 document this methodological relationship without creating a formal CRMinf dependency.

---

### 5.3 Complementary use of CRMinf

CRMinf may be used as a complementary model when a dataset or research project requires a more detailed representation of:

* chains of scholarly argumentation;
* explicit inference-making activities;
* premises and conclusions;
* belief adoption;
* competing or dependent arguments;
* inference logic; or
* detailed provenance of reasoning processes.

EKCD's interpretive-assertion pattern is therefore not intended to replace CRMinf.

Instead, it provides a comparatively lightweight layer for evidence-traceable interpretation in datasets where full argumentation modeling would introduce unnecessary complexity.

## 6. Deprecation and migration of legacy values

The following v1.1.x values are retained in v1.2.0 for backward reference but are deprecated for new data:

* `ekcd:Direct`
* `ekcd:Probable`
* `ekcd:Contextual`

Each is marked with:

```turtle
owl:deprecated "true"^^xsd:boolean
```

These values were previously used as values of `ekcd:hasInterpretiveConfidence`, but they combined information about interpretive basis and confidence in ways that are not semantically equivalent to the two independent dimensions introduced in v1.2.0.

Accordingly, **no automatic one-to-one migration is asserted**.

In particular:

```text
ekcd:Direct     ≠ ekcd:HighConfidence
ekcd:Probable   ≠ ekcd:MediumConfidence
ekcd:Contextual ≠ ekcd:LowConfidence
```

Likewise, the following should be understood as likely basis-oriented correspondences rather than complete automatic conversions:

```text
ekcd:Direct
→ typically reassess as ekcd:DirectEvidenceBasis
  + independently assign confidence

ekcd:Probable
→ typically reassess whether the assertion is inferential
  + independently assign confidence

ekcd:Contextual
→ typically reassess as ekcd:ContextualBasis
  + independently assign confidence
```

Existing ABox data using the legacy values does not need to be rewritten immediately.

When such data is revised or reused, however, each interpretive assertion should preferably be reassessed individually and supplied with separate values for:

```turtle
ekcd:hasInterpretiveBasis
```

and:

```turtle
ekcd:hasInterpretiveConfidence
```

This migration policy avoids introducing unwarranted epistemic judgments through mechanical conversion.

## 7. Compatibility

EKCD v1.2.0 is designed as a non-breaking minor release.

The release:

* retains the existing EKCD namespace;
* removes no existing class IRI;
* removes no existing object-property IRI;
* removes no existing datatype-property IRI;
* removes no existing annotation-property IRI;
* retains the legacy `ekcd:Direct`, `ekcd:Probable`, and `ekcd:Contextual` IRIs;
* introduces new vocabulary for the two-axis interpretive model; and
* adds explanatory annotations concerning CRMinf without introducing a formal dependency.

Existing ABox data produced against v1.1.5 should therefore remain syntactically usable with v1.2.0.

Semantic migration is recommended only for ABox assertions that use the deprecated legacy confidence values.

No blanket automated replacement of legacy values is recommended.

---

## 8. Release classification

EKCD v1.2.0 is classified as a **minor release** because it introduces a non-breaking expansion and refinement of the ontology vocabulary.

The release adds:

* one OWL class;
* one OWL object property;
* three controlled interpretive-basis values;
* three controlled epistemic-confidence values; and
* additional ontology and term annotations.

It also deprecates, without removing, three legacy controlled values.

The revision does not constitute a major-version change because the EKCD namespace, existing principal classes and properties, and overall compatibility with previously created ABox data are preserved.

The principal semantic change is a refinement of the interpretive-assertion model rather than a replacement of the EKCD ontology architecture.

## 9. Summary of the v1.2.0 modeling change

The central change from v1.1.5 to v1.2.0 can be summarized as follows.

### v1.1.5

```text
InterpretiveAssertion
└─ hasInterpretiveConfidence
   ├─ Direct
   ├─ Probable
   └─ Contextual
```

### v1.2.0

```text
InterpretiveAssertion
├─ hasInterpretiveBasis
│  ├─ DirectEvidenceBasis
│  ├─ InferentialBasis
│  └─ ContextualBasis
│
└─ hasInterpretiveConfidence
   ├─ HighConfidence
   ├─ MediumConfidence
   └─ LowConfidence
```

The v1.2.0 model therefore distinguishes **the basis of an interpretation** from **the confidence assigned to that interpretation**, while retaining lightweight provenance through `prov:wasDerivedFrom`.

This refinement is intended to improve the transparency, traceability, and analytical precision of semantic modeling for historical and digital-humanities research.
